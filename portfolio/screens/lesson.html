The Pin Factory and the Baker's Self-Interest

The big idea

In this lesson you will learn how dividing work into small tasks multiplies what people can make, and why everyday trade runs on self-interest rather than kindness.

The pin factory

In the chapter on the division of labour, Adam Smith makes a bold claim about work itself. The greatest improvements in what labour can produce, he says, seem to be the result of one thing: dividing the work into parts.

It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker, that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest.

— The Wealth of Nations

His chosen example is the pin-maker. A worker who has never been trained could hardly make a single pin in a day, and certainly could not make twenty.
